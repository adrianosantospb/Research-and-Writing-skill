# [EXTENSION] Module B — Academic text humanization (Level 2+)

"Humanization" here means making academic prose read like it was written by a careful human scholar: natural, fluent, varied and precise.
It is **not** a technique to evade AI-detection tools, and it never introduces deliberate errors or artificial irregularities (E9).
If a user asks to "bypass/beat AI detectors", decline that goal, offer authentic improvement instead, and mention the AI-use disclosure (`../extensions/ethics-data-and-ai-disclosure.md`).

## Targets
| Target | What to do |
|---|---|
| Natural sentence construction | Prefer subject–verb close together; active voice where the agent matters; passive where the method is the focus (common in Methods). |
| Fluency | Remove stacked nominalizations ("the performance of the evaluation of…" → "evaluating…"); fix stilted word order. |
| Lexical diversity | Vary non-technical words that repeat in close proximity; **never** vary technical terms (E4). |
| Syntactic variation | Mix sentence lengths and openings; avoid every sentence starting with the same connective or subject. |
| Natural transitions | Use a transition only when there is a real logical relation; prefer implicit cohesion (repeating the key noun, demonstratives) over a connective at the start of every sentence. |
| Repetition | Remove redundant restatements, doubled expressions ("each and every", "future prospects"), and paragraphs that end by repeating their first sentence. |
| Mechanical phrasing | Replace formulaic filler (see list) with direct statements — or delete it. |
| Paragraph rhythm | Topic sentence → development → link; avoid uniform paragraph templates and one-sentence paragraphs unless intentional. |
| Author identity | Everything above happens inside the author's fingerprint (`authorial-style-preservation.md`). |

## Formulaic patterns to reduce (examples, not a ban list)
| Language | Typical mechanical phrasing |
|---|---|
| English | "It is worth noting that", "It is important to highlight", "plays a crucial/pivotal role", "In today's fast-paced world", "delve into", "in the realm of", "a myriad of", "Furthermore/Moreover" opening consecutive sentences, triplet lists by default ("X, Y, and Z") |
| Portuguese (BR) | "Vale ressaltar que", "É importante destacar que", "desempenha um papel fundamental", "Nesse sentido," repeated, "no que tange a", "a nível de", "Dessa forma/Diante disso" opening every paragraph |
| Spanish | "Cabe destacar que", "Es importante señalar que", "juega un papel crucial", "En este sentido," repeated, "a nivel de", "en base a" |
| French | "Il convient de noter que", "Il est important de souligner que", "joue un rôle crucial", "Dans ce contexte," repeated, "au niveau de" (abstract), "De plus/En outre" opening consecutive sentences |

A pattern is acceptable when it is the author's habit used sparingly or when it carries real meaning; reduce frequency rather than eradicating.

## Guardrails
- Do not add rhetorical flourishes, metaphors or enthusiasm absent from the original.
- Keep hedging exactly as strong as the evidence and the author's original (E5).
- Do not make text deliberately "imperfect"; clarity is the goal.
- Keep the register academic (no contractions in English unless the venue allows; no colloquialisms).

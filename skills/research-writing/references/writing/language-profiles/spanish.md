# [EXTENSION] Language profile — Spanish (es-ES and Latin American Spanish)

Reference norms: RAE and ASALE (*Nueva gramática*, *Ortografía de la lengua española* 2010, *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas*). Examples are in Spanish because they illustrate Spanish usage.
Variant: see `../multilingual-editing.md` Step 2; ask when there are no cues. Never mix variants.

## 1. Subjunctive vs. indicative
- Subjunctive after volition, necessity, possibility, emotion, doubt and purpose: *es necesario que*, *es posible que*, *para que*, *antes de que*, *no creo que* + subjunctive; *creo que*, *es evidente que* + indicative.
- *aunque* + indicative (asserted fact) / + subjunctive (hypothetical or conceded): meaning differs → `[AUTHOR CHECK]` when unclear.
- *después de que*: subjunctive is required for future reference and common for past; indicative is also accepted for past facts — keep consistent.
- *el hecho de que* admits both; subjunctive is more frequent in academic prose.
- Conditional sentences: *si* + imperfect subjunctive → conditional (*si se aumentara el tamaño, se obtendría…*); never *si* + conditional.

## 2. Tense consistency (concordancia de tiempos)
- Past main verb → imperfect subjunctive in the subordinate: *Se pidió que los participantes completaran el cuestionario*.
- Present/future main verb → present subjunctive: *Se recomienda que se utilice…*.
- **Regional**: es-ES uses the *pretérito perfecto compuesto* for past linked to the present (*en este trabajo se ha propuesto*); Latin American Spanish prefers the simple past (*se propuso*). Follow the chosen variant consistently.
- Methods and results in past; established knowledge and the paper's contribution in present.

## 3. Agreement and prepositions
- Subject–verb and noun–adjective agreement across long noun phrases (*la mayoría de los modelos evaluados obtuvo/obtuvieron* — both accepted; be consistent).
- **Dequeísmo / queísmo**: *pienso que* (not *de que*); *estar seguro de que*, *darse cuenta de que*, *a pesar de que* (not *que* alone).
- Prefer *con base en* / *sobre la base de* / *a partir de* over *en base a*.
- *de acuerdo con*, *con respecto a / respecto de*, *en relación con* (preferred to *en relación a*), *a pesar de*.
- *leísmo*: *le* for a masculine person as direct object is accepted by RAE; *laísmo* and *loísmo* are errors.

## 4. Idiomatic academic expressions
- Avoid the *gerundio de posterioridad*: *Se entrenó el modelo, obteniendo un 90 %* → *Se entrenó el modelo y se obtuvo un 90 %*.
- Prefer *se* impersonal/passive (*se analizaron los datos*) to periphrastic passive overuse (*los datos fueron analizados*); *nosotros* (plural of modesty) is acceptable — keep the author's choice.
- Calques to fix: *jugar un rol/papel* → *desempeñar un papel*; *aplicar para* → *solicitar*; *asumir* meaning "suppose" → *suponer*; *a nivel de* (non-literal) → *en el ámbito de*, *en cuanto a*; *en orden a* → *para*; *remarcar* → *destacar, subrayar*; *colapsar* (figurative overuse); *evidencia* in the sense of "proof" is accepted in many varieties but *prueba(s)* is clearer in es-ES.
- *Cabe destacar que*, *es importante señalar que*: reduce frequency (see `../academic-humanization.md`).

## 5. Orthography and typography (OLE 2010)
- *solo* and demonstratives without accent; *guion*, *truhan*; accents are mandatory on capital letters (*Índice*, *ÉXITO*).
- Prefixes attached to single-word bases (*exdirector*, *posgrado*, *antivirus*), separated before multiword bases (*ex primer ministro*), hyphen before capital letters/numbers (*anti-OTAN*, *sub-21*).
- Opening question and exclamation marks are required (*¿Qué factores…?*).
- No comma between subject and verb; comma after discourse markers (*sin embargo,*, *por lo tanto,*).
- Quotation marks: RAE recommends « » first (common in es-ES), then “ ”, then ‘ ’; Latin American texts usually use “ ”. Follow venue/variant.
- Lowercase for months, weekdays, languages and nationalities; titles in sentence case (only first word and proper names capitalized).
- Numbers: RAE accepts decimal comma or point; es-ES commonly uses comma, Mexico and several Latin American countries use point — follow venue/variant consistently. Thousands separated by a (thin) space in numbers of five or more digits (*10 000*), not by point or comma. *%* is written with a space (*15 %*) per OLE 2010; follow the venue if it differs.

## 6. Terminological consistency
- One Spanish term per concept; keep established English terms if the author does (*machine learning* vs *aprendizaje automático*) but do not alternate.
- Regional technical vocabulary: es-ES *ordenador*, *fichero/archivo*, *móvil*; Latin America *computadora/computador*, *archivo*, *celular*. Choose by variant.
- Acronyms: Spanish acronyms when established (*IA*, *ONU*), otherwise the original with expansion at first use; acronyms do not take a written plural *s* (*las ONG*).

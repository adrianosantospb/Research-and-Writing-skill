# Method

Rules: R14–R19, C1, C2, C4. Purpose: describe the proposed solution and how it was evaluated. **No results here (R19).**

## 3.1 Proposed solution (R14)
Describe: architecture (include a figure), main components, algorithms (pseudocode when novel), frameworks, technologies, implementation details (versions, configurations, hardware when relevant to reproducibility).
Close by explaining **how the solution addresses the technical problem stated in the Introduction** (C1).

## 3.2 Research methodology (R15)
Classify the research using `../methodology-taxonomy.md` and write it as **continuous academic prose**, justifying each classification. Example pattern:
> "This is an empirical, original study with explanatory objectives. It combines laboratory procedures, … under a quantitative approach, since …"

## 3.3 Models and frameworks (R16)
Standards, frameworks, conceptual models, evaluation models, software engineering models adopted — each with citation and why it was chosen.

## 3.4 Experimental planning (R17, R18)
Follow `../experimental-planning.md`: context selection, hypotheses (H0/H1), variables, experimental units, design, instrumentation, threats to validity (`../threats-to-validity.md`).

[EXTENSION] Non-experimental designs (systematic reviews, qualitative studies, design science, case studies) — see `../extensions/` for the equivalent planning items. Keep the same principle: plan in Method, report in Results, interpret in Discussion.

[EXTENSION] When human participants or personal data are involved, add ethics approval and data handling — see `../extensions/ethics-data-and-ai-disclosure.md`.

## Common mistakes
- Reporting accuracy numbers or "the experiment showed…" in the Method.
- Methodology as a bulleted list of labels with no justification.
- Hypotheses that are not testable (no measurable dependent variable).
- Missing information needed to reproduce the study.

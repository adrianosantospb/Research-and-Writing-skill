# [EXTENSION] Qualitative, mixed-method and design-oriented studies

The guideline's experimental planning (§3.4) targets controlled experiments. For other designs keep the same **plan → report → interpret** separation and adapt the planning items:

| §3.4 item | Qualitative / case study equivalent | Design science equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Context selection | Case/site selection and rationale | Problem environment and stakeholders |
| Hypotheses | Research questions / propositions | Design requirements and evaluation criteria |
| Variables | Constructs/themes of interest | Artifact properties to evaluate |
| Experimental units | Participants, sampling (purposive, theoretical), saturation criterion | Evaluation episodes, users |
| Design | Data collection protocol (interviews, observation, documents) | Build–evaluate cycles |
| Instrumentation | Interview guide, coding scheme, QDA software | Prototype, evaluation instruments |
| Analysis | Thematic analysis, grounded theory, content analysis — with coding procedure | Evaluation methods (experiment, case, survey) |

## Validity / trustworthiness
Keep the four threat types when the venue expects them, or use credibility, transferability, dependability and confirmability, with strategies (triangulation, member checking, audit trail, inter-coder agreement, thick description).

## Mixed methods
State the design (convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential) and **how and where** quantitative and qualitative strands are integrated.

## Results vs Discussion in qualitative work
Results = themes/categories with representative (anonymized) quotes and frequencies; Discussion = interpretation and comparison with literature. Quotes come only from the author's data (I2).

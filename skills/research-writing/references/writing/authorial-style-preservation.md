# [EXTENSION] Academic writing fingerprint preservation (all levels)

Purpose: improve the text without erasing the author's intellectual and stylistic identity (E11). Two authors edited with this skill should still sound like two different people.

## Step 1 — Build the style profile (before editing)
Read at least two representative paragraphs (ideally from different sections) and note:

| Dimension | What to record |
|---|---|
| Lexical preferences | Favorite verbs/connectives, preferred terms for key concepts, words the author consistently avoids |
| Sentence complexity | Typical length range; use of subordination vs. coordination; parentheses and lists |
| Argumentative style | Assertive vs. cautious; deductive (claim first) vs. inductive (evidence first); use of rhetorical questions |
| Paragraph structure | Typical length; topic-sentence habit; how paragraphs end |
| Academic voice | First person singular/plural, impersonal constructions (pt *se*/passive, es *se* impersonal, fr *on*/*nous*), "the author" |
| Preferred terminology | Glossary of defined terms, acronyms, capitalization choices |
| Formality | Degree of formality; contractions; colloquial markers |
| Citation integration | Narrative ("Silva (2023) shows") vs. parenthetical; reporting verbs used |

Keep the profile internal; include a 2–3 line summary in the editing report at Level 2–3.

## Step 2 — Edit inside the profile
- Keep the author's voice and person; do not switch *we* → passive or *nós* → *se* across the text.
- Keep preferred terms and spelling variant; unify only real inconsistencies.
- Stay within the author's sentence-length range (Level 2: small adjustments; Level 3: may split/merge, but keep the overall texture).
- Keep the author's argument order unless Level 3 restructuring is clearly needed; then explain.
- Keep characteristic constructions that are correct, even if uncommon.

## Latitude by level
| Level | Latitude |
|---|---|
| 1 | Change only errors; profile untouched. |
| 2 | Adjust wording and rhythm; preserve voice, terminology, argument order and paragraphing. |
| 3 | Restructure where it improves clarity; still preserve voice, terminology, formality and argumentative stance. |

## Multi-author or inconsistent documents
- If sections clearly come from different authors, ask whether to harmonize. Without an answer, harmonize only terminology, variant and typography, not voice.
- When the author's style itself causes errors (e.g., overlong sentences that become ambiguous), fix the error with the smallest change and mention the pattern once in the report.

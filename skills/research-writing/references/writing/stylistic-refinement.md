# [EXTENSION] Module D — Academic stylistic refinement (Level 2 sentence level, Level 3 paragraph level)

Complements the style rules W1–W5 in `../core-rules.md` (not repeated here). Never introduce unsupported claims or change scientific arguments (E5, E6).

## Sentence level (Level 2 and 3)
- **Precision**: replace vague words with the exact term the author already uses elsewhere ("good results" → the metric the paper reports; "several" → the number, if stated in the text). Do not invent numbers.
- **Concision**: cut empty phrases ("in order to" → "to"; "*com o objetivo de*" → "*para*"; "*con el fin de*" → "*para*"; "*afin de*" stays when needed), redundant pairs and doubled hedges ("may possibly").
- **Restructuring**: split sentences over ~40 words with more than two ideas; merge choppy consecutive sentences sharing the subject; put the main information in the main clause; given → new information order.
- **Register**: academic but readable; avoid colloquialisms and emphatic adverbs ("very", "extremely", "totally") unless they carry measured meaning.
- **Terminology**: one term per concept throughout (E4); build a glossary while editing; when the author alternates between two terms for the same concept, unify to the more frequent/defined one and report it.

## Paragraph level (Level 3 only)
- One main idea per paragraph, stated in the topic sentence.
- Logical order inside the paragraph: claim → evidence/citation → interpretation → link to the next idea.
- **Cohesion**: explicit logical connectives where the relation is real (contrast, cause, consequence, addition, concession); referential cohesion (consistent key nouns, clear pronoun antecedents).
- **Coherence across paragraphs**: each paragraph's first sentence connects to the previous one; reorder sentences or paragraphs inside a section when it clarifies the argument; report every move.
- **Redundancy**: merge paragraphs that make the same point; remove restatements of the previous section's conclusion unless used as a deliberate bridge.
- **Section discipline** (from the guideline): keep interpretation out of Results (R20) and results out of Method (R19). If editing reveals a violation, flag it rather than silently moving content across sections.

## Readability checks
- Average sentence length reasonable for the field and language (academic prose often 20–30 words; adapt to the author's profile).
- Acronyms defined at first use (W1), not redefined.
- Lists parallel in grammar; figures/tables referenced before they appear (W4).

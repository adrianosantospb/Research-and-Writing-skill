# [EXTENSION] Language profile — Academic English (en-US and en-GB)

Reference points: the venue's house style first; then APA/Chicago/IEEE/ACM style guides; Merriam-Webster (US) or Oxford (UK) dictionaries.

## 1. Articles and countability
- Uncountable in academic use: *research, evidence, information, feedback, knowledge, software, equipment, advice, progress* → no *a/an*, no plural (*a research* → *a study* / *research*; *researches* → *studies*).
- *the* for specific, previously mentioned or unique referents (*the proposed method*, *the dataset described in Section 3*); zero article for general plural/uncountable nouns (*Deep learning requires data*).
- Acronyms take the article of their pronunciation (*an SVM*, *a CNN*, *an LLM*).

## 2. Subject–verb agreement
- *The number of participants was…* vs *A number of participants were…*; *each/every* + singular; *none* singular or plural (be consistent); *data* singular or plural per venue, consistently; *criteria/phenomena* are plural.
- Long subjects: agree with the head noun (*The accuracy of the three models was…*).
- Collective nouns: singular in US English; singular or plural in UK English — consistent.

## 3. Tense consistency (common conventions)
| Context | Typical tense |
|---|---|
| What the paper does / its contribution | Present (*This paper proposes…*) |
| Established knowledge | Present (*Transformers rely on attention*) |
| Specific prior studies | Past or present (*Silva et al. (2023) reported / report*) — choose one |
| Research trend | Present perfect (*Several studies have shown*) |
| Method and Results | Past (*We collected…*, *Accuracy reached…*) |
| Discussion / interpretation | Present (*These results suggest…*) |

## 4. Prepositions and collocations
- *based on* (not *in*), *consist of*, *composed of* / *comprises* (avoid *comprised of* in formal text), *depend on*, *focus on*, *result in* (effect) / *result from* (cause), *in contrast to/with*, *compared with/to*, *different from* (*than* in US informal), *according to*, *in terms of* (sparingly).
- Verb–noun collocations: *conduct/carry out an experiment, study, survey* (not *make/do a research*); *achieve accuracy*; *draw conclusions*; *pose/raise a question*; *address a problem*; *fill a gap*; *yield results*; *gain insight*.

## 5. Grammar points that recur in L2 academic English
- Dangling modifiers: *Using the dataset, the accuracy was measured* → *Using the dataset, we measured the accuracy*.
- *allow/enable/permit* + object + *to*: *allows us to estimate* (not *allows to estimate*).
- *It was possible to observe that* → *We observed that*.
- Restrictive *that* vs non-restrictive *, which* (required in US style; UK accepts restrictive *which*).
- Parallelism in lists and comparisons.
- False friends for Portuguese/Spanish/French writers: *actually* (≠ *atualmente/actualmente/actuellement* → *currently*), *eventually* (≠ *eventualmente* → *possibly, occasionally*), *realize* (≠ *realizar/réaliser* → *carry out, perform*), *assist* (≠ *assistir* → *attend, watch*), *pretend* (≠ *pretender/prétendre* → *intend, claim*), *sensible* (≠ *sensível/sensible* → *sensitive*), *library* (≠ *livraria/librería*), *in this sense* (→ *in this regard*, or delete).

## 6. Academic vocabulary and register
- Reporting verbs graded by strength (keep the author's strength, E5): *suggest < indicate < show < demonstrate < prove*.
- Avoid contractions, *a lot of*, *very/really*, *etc.* in running text (venue-dependent), *in order to* (→ *to*).
- Formulaic phrases to reduce: see `../academic-humanization.md`.

## 7. US vs UK conventions
| Item | en-US | en-GB |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | *-ize, -or, -er, analyze, modeling, labeled* | *-ise* (or Oxford *-ize*), *-our, -re, analyse, modelling, labelled* |
| Quotation marks | “double”, period/comma inside | ‘single’, punctuation outside unless part of the quote |
| *e.g., i.e.* | followed by a comma | comma optional |
| Serial (Oxford) comma | common (APA, Chicago require) | optional (follow style) |
| Dates | *November 10, 2026* | *10 November 2026* |
Never mix within a document; venue style overrides.

## 8. Numbers
- Decimal point; leading zero (*0.05*) except where the style omits it for values that cannot exceed 1 (APA: *p = .03*).
- Numbers below 10 in words in running text unless with units or in a series of measurements (APA/Chicago variants — follow the style).
- Space between number and unit (*5 ms*); *%* without space in most English styles (*15%*).

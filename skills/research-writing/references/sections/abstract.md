# Abstract (+ categories, general terms, keywords)

Rules: R01, R02, C7. Write the abstract **last**, after the body exists.

## Required content (in this order, one or two sentences each)
1. **Context** — the research area in one sentence.
2. **Problem and motivation** — what problem and why it is relevant/interesting.
3. **Proposed approach** — what the solution does (not how it is built in detail).
4. **Evaluation method** — how it was evaluated (design, data/participants, metrics).
5. **Main results** — the key quantitative or qualitative findings (with numbers if available).
6. **Conclusion** — what the results mean and the main contribution.

## Checks before delivering
- Self-contained: no citations, no undefined acronyms, no references to figures/sections.
- Every claim exists in the body (C7). No results that are not in the Results section.
- Respect the venue word limit (ask if unknown; typical 150–250 words for articles; ABNT NBR 6028 for Brazilian academic works: 150–500 words for theses/dissertations/TCC, 100–250 for articles — confirm with the institution).

## Subject categories, general terms, keywords
- Categories follow the venue's classification system (e.g., ACM CCS for ACM venues, IEEE taxonomy, MeSH for health). Ask which system; do not invent category codes — use `[CATEGORY PENDING: ACM CCS]` if unknown.
- General terms only if the venue requires them.
- Keywords: 3–6 terms covering the main concept(s), method(s) and application domain. Avoid repeating title words only.

## Common mistakes
- Abstract that is only motivation, with no results.
- Results without numbers when numbers exist.
- Promising contributions the paper never demonstrates.

## Skeleton
> [Context]. [Problem], which [consequence/relevance]. This [article/report/dissertation] proposes [solution], which [what it does]. We evaluated it through [evaluation design] with [data/participants]. Results show [main finding with metric]. [Conclusion / contribution].

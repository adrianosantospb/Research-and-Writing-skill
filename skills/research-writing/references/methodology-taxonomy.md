# Research methodology taxonomy (Method §3.2)

Rule R15: classify on all six dimensions, justify each choice, and write the result as **continuous academic prose**.
The categories come from the guideline; the short definitions and decision questions are helper text.

| Dimension | Options | Decision question |
|---|---|---|
| **Research area** | Theoretical · Methodological · Empirical · Practical | Does the work build theory, propose a method, collect evidence from observation/experiment, or solve a concrete practical problem? |
| **Research nature** | Original scientific research · Survey or review research | Does it produce new results, or organize/synthesize existing literature? |
| **Research objectives** | Exploratory · Descriptive · Explanatory | Is the goal to get familiar with a little-known problem, to describe characteristics of a phenomenon, or to identify causes/effects and explain why? |
| **Research procedures** | Field research · Document-based research | Is data gathered where the phenomenon occurs, or from existing documents/records? |
| **Research object** | Bibliographic · Laboratory · Field | Is the object studied through literature, under controlled conditions, or in its natural setting? |
| **Research approach** | Quantitative · Qualitative · Mixed-method | Is the evidence numerical/statistical, interpretive/textual, or both integrated? |

## Short definitions
- **Theoretical**: develops or refines concepts, models or theories.
- **Methodological**: proposes or evaluates methods, processes or tools for doing research or engineering.
- **Empirical**: produces evidence through observation, measurement or experimentation.
- **Practical**: applies knowledge to solve a specific real-world problem.
- **Original**: produces new knowledge or artifacts. **Survey/review**: synthesizes existing work (for systematic reviews see `extensions/systematic-review.md`).
- **Exploratory**: builds familiarity, generates hypotheses. **Descriptive**: characterizes a population/phenomenon. **Explanatory**: tests causal or explanatory relationships (typically with hypotheses).
- **Quantitative**: measurement and statistical analysis. **Qualitative**: meaning, interpretation, coding of non-numerical data. **Mixed**: integrates both with an explicit integration strategy.

## Consistency checks
- Explanatory objectives normally imply hypotheses (H0/H1) → the Method must contain them (R17).
- Quantitative approach → statistical tests and conclusion-validity discussion (R18).
- Survey/review nature → no "proposed solution" in the experimental sense; use the review protocol instead.
- Laboratory object → control variables and instrumentation must be described.

## Prose template
> "Regarding its area, this research is [option], since [reason]. By nature, it is [option] because [reason]. Its objectives are [option], as it seeks to [goal]. As for procedures, it is [option], [reason], and its object is [option], [reason]. Finally, it adopts a [option] approach, [reason]."
Rewrite naturally — avoid producing a mechanical list in sentence form.
